## 1. Event planning end to end ("run events")

### Takeaway
The rules are real, public, numerous and spread over at least five documents that disagree with each other on some lead times; there is no single official timeline or checklist. This is the best substrate for a main feature: a deterministic "describe the event, get the back-planned permit timeline with citations". Evidence of officer complaints about event approval specifically is still thin; the evidence is structural (complexity and contradictions), not testimonial.

### Cited Findings
**Is there one official timeline? No.**
- The Handbook's only general guidance is that reservation requests should be made "generally at least 3-6 weeks in advance", and that "Trojan Event Services has the requested date deadlines on their website". READ — [Student Handbook 2026-27, Appendix V](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- The TES Forms and Permits page lists seven separate forms (Early Booking Application, Certificate of Liability Insurance, Change of Use/Temporary Event Permit, FPM Checklist, Fundraising Approval Request, LAFD Division 5 Permit, USC Event Permit Application) with no lead times next to any of them. FETCHED — [TES Forms and Permits](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/forms-and-permits/)
- Official sources contradict each other: decorations need approval "at least three weeks prior" in the Terms and Conditions (READ — [TES T&C 2026](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/TES-Terms-and-Conditions-2026.pdf)) but "at least 2 weeks" in the FAQ (FETCHED — [TES FAQ](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/frequently-asked-questions/)); large-venue cancellation is "at least three weeks" in the T&C but "at least 4 weeks" in the FAQ. The UEPA link printed in the T&C (adminopsnet.usc.edu) differs from the one on the Forms page (usc-its.my.site.com/eventpermits). The classroom request form has two different Wufoo URLs on the TES FAQ and the Campus Activities RSO Resources page.

**Violations.** The "RSO Violation Report" is not a statistics report. It is a one-page TES staff intake checklist ("As of 8/2025") for reporting a possible RSO event violation to a named TES staff member. It lists 23 tick-box categories (including "Other") in three groups — Registration and Compliance (event not registered/reserved properly; exceeded capacity; ran more than 45 minutes over; "fronting"; failure to hire required security or staff; unapproved flyers; unapproved marketing; unpaid TES fees; inaccurate event details; no-show/late cancellation), Risk and Safety (alcohol without approval; smoking/vaping; unapproved food; fire-code breaches; damage; unsafe behaviour; excessive trash), Conduct (DPS involvement; altercations; hazing; bias complaints; repeat violations) — and a referral choice between "Campus Activities advisement/education only" and "Referral to OCE for possible policy investigation". READ — [TES RSO Violation Report](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RSO-Violation-Report.pdf)
- Consequences stated: violations "may result in a written warning, additional fees ... and/or a loss of all Trojan Event Services privileges" (READ — [Handbook App. V](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)); a suspected sales/sponsorship violation means the event "may be charged off-campus rates for the venue, and/or be canceled" with deposits forfeited (READ — [T&C §Q](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/TES-Terms-and-Conditions-2026.pdf)).

**What EngageSC does here.** EngageSC holds event listings and an approval status; the public RSS feed exposes about 60 fields per event including cohostingGroupNames, eventFlyerFullUrl, foodProvided, attendanceEstimate, approvalStatus and locationType, with many locations hidden behind sign-in. FETCHED — [EngageSC events RSS](https://engage.usc.edu/rss_events). Venue booking and permits live outside EngageSC (TES reservation form, UEPA portal, Wufoo classroom form).

### Inferences
- The introduction of a formal violation intake form in August 2025 suggests TES is actively documenting RSO non-compliance; a planner that prevents the ten "Registration and Compliance" items is something TES and Campus Activities could endorse, not just clubs. No frequency data exists publicly, so "most common violations" cannot be stated; the checklist shows what staff look for.
- Because sources conflict (2 vs 3 weeks; 3 vs 4 weeks), the product should apply the stricter number, show both citations, and flag "sources disagree". That is a credible accuracy story for the 25% technical criterion.
- Longest-lead items define the planner's branches: social dance (3 months), minors (60 days), USG travel (9 academic weeks), USG vendor purchases (6 academic weeks), GSG (6-8 weeks), dance form (5 weeks), final payment (21 days), A/V and rentals (3 weeks), UEPA/DPS/fundraising/alcohol/props/FMS (2 weeks).
- Hard calendar blocks the planner can enforce in code: first three weeks of each semester, study days and finals, alcohol only Thu-Sat, tabling only Mon-Fri 10-2, home football days (no outdoor reservations).

### Gaps
- Film-screening public-performance licensing rules for RSOs: not found in the T&C, FAQ or Handbook. Contract-signing authority for RSOs and a domestic travel policy: not found (the Handbook only has a "Student-Led International Travel" policy). Treat all three as "ask a Campus Activities mentor".
- DPS staffing ratios and fees are discretionary and unpublished; the only number found is a 2022-23 FSL guide's "1 guard for every 40 guests" (SNIPPET — [FSL Event Hosting Education Program](https://greeklife.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/FINAL-USC-FSLD-Event-Hosting-Education-Program-2022-2023-DO-NOT-EDIT.pdf)), which should not be encoded as a rule.
- The "Outdoor Events and Social Dance Policy" referenced by the Handbook and the FPM checklist were not opened. USC academic-calendar dates (needed for "academic weeks", study days, finals) were not fetched.
- Still no 2025-26 article in which officers complain about event approval specifically.

## 2. Money beyond USG funding

### Takeaway
The most useful new finding is on sponsorship and fundraising law, not on more grant sources: the 2026-27 Handbook bans monetary corporate sponsorship, sponsorship activations, paid raffles and brand ambassadors for RSOs. Other funding sources exist but are fragmented by school, and the GSG rulebook is behind a login, so a full "which fund am I eligible for" engine cannot be completed from public data alone.

### Cited Findings
- Corporate sponsorships of RSO events "can only be in-kind donations ... and may not include monetary donations"; RSOs may not bring a business to campus as a vendor unless a pre-existing event is occurring; co-sponsorship with unrecognised student organisations is "strictly prohibited"; the RSO must be primary contact, payer and primary name on all advertising. READ — [Handbook "RSO Co-Sponsorship"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- "The university does not permit RSOs to engage in sponsorship activations"; only USG and GSG may, limited to two per semester, through a Sponsorship Form on the Campus Activities site. READ — [Handbook "Sponsorship Activation"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- Raffles requiring a purchase or donation are illegal in California; "free drawings" are allowed only with specific ticket wording and the word "raffle" should not be used; silent auctions must work with University Advancement. READ — [Handbook "Raffles or Lotteries", "Free Drawings", "Silent Auction"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- RSOs may receive tax-deductible donations only through the university (gift account via University Advancement, arranged through Campus Activities); an RSO that files for its own tax-exempt status without approval loses recognition. Charity collections need a letter on the non-profit's letterhead plus proof of tax-exempt status uploaded to the UEPA portal. Ticket sales are allowed only to cover that event's expenses. READ — [Handbook RSO chapter item 14 and "Sales and Fundraising by RSOs"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- RSOs must "maintain a student-led process for independently accounting for RSO funding and expenses"; banking is via the USC Credit Union "and/or through Campus Activities". READ — [Handbook Recognition Requirements, Benefits](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf). Credit Union forms named: "New Account Package" and "Change Authorized Signers". FETCHED — [Campus Activities RSO Resources](https://campusactivities.usc.edu/programs/recognized-student-organizations/rso-resources/)
- Dues "may not be a requirement for any level of membership" (constitution template, twice), although the same template lets a club state a per-semester dues amount members "may be asked" to pay. READ — [2026-27 Constitution Template](https://campusactivities.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026-27-RSO-Constitution-Template-1.pdf)
- GSG: applications due "6-8 weeks before your event date" depending on type; a designated officer must watch the orientation and pass a Finance Assessment to unlock the EngageSC application; events must be open to all graduate students; five supporting documents (vendor list, Canva flyer template, Constituency Organization Approval Memo, Transportation Waiver, Prize Recipient Form). FETCHED — [GSG Funding for RSOs](https://gsg.usc.edu/gsg-funding/). The RSO Funding Guidebook link resolves to a Google Doc that returned HTTP 401 without a login, so caps and ineligible items remain unverified.
- GSG publishes a "GSG-approved vendors list" as a Google Sheet (not opened). FETCHED — [GSG Event Planning Resources](https://gsg.usc.edu/resources/event-planning-resources/)
- USG and Campus Activities run an "FMS RSO Cost Waiver Fund"; the page gives no amounts, eligibility or deadline, and its "Apply here" text has no visible link. FETCHED — [USG RSO resources](https://usg.usc.edu/resources/rsos/)
- School-level sources (all SNIPPET, not opened): Viterbi "Student Organization Support (SOS)" is endowment-based, supplementary and described as the last option — [Viterbi funding Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1sMtp7y_MBsgyn0iitBJ4-g2jwlSEFOk9FmF7avYu0ac/export?format=pdf); Marshall requires all MRSO event expenses to be approved and paid in advance, no reimbursements — [Marshall Student Organization Resources](https://students.marshall.usc.edu/node/3081); Rossier's RSO council has its own finance chair — [Rossier RSOC](https://sites.google.com/view/usc-rsoc/rso-funding). USG's own page tells applicants to explore "other funding sources throughout USC" (SNIPPET — [USG Funding](https://usg.usc.edu/branches/funding/)).

### Inferences
- A "sponsorship and fundraising legality check" is a cheap, high-value small feature: clubs routinely seek cash sponsors and run raffles, and both are prohibited as written. The same check covers payment collection (TES/DPS approval for cash at events) and charity letters.
- A "fund finder" can be built as a short questionnaire (undergrad/grad mix, school affiliation, event type, date) that returns USG board, GSG, school council, FMS waiver, gift account and in-kind sponsorship, but only USG rules are fully encodable today; the rest should link out with "rules not public" labels.
- There is no documented "which fund am I eligible for" complaint; the problem is inferred from fragmentation.

### Gaps
- GSG caps, eligible expenses and payment method (guidebook login-gated). Programming-assembly and school-council amounts. Whether payment apps (Venmo, Zelle) may be used to collect dues: no rule found beyond USG's ban on peer-to-peer vendor payments (prior notes).
- University Advancement gift-account lead times and minimums: not found.

## 3. Branding and marketing compliance

### Takeaway
Flyer and merchandise rules are specific, mechanical and publicly documented, and several are checkable from an image: a vision-based flyer/merch pre-check is feasible, visually strong in a demo, and not offered by EngageSC. The Handbook itself contains two different mandatory non-discrimination wordings, which the tool should surface rather than hide.

### Cited Findings
- Every printed or digital event advertisement must include: full name of the sponsoring organisation; time, date and place; any entrance fee; the text "Events are open to all USC Students. [Organization] operates its programs in accordance with USC's Notice of Non-Discrimination."; and an accommodations contact line; materials must comply with the Digital Accessibility Policy. READ — [Handbook Appendix IX](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- A different wording is required elsewhere in the same Handbook and in the constitution template for "all promotional materials": "[RSO] programs and events are open to all eligible individuals. [RSO] operates all of its programs and activities consistent with the USC Notice of Non-Discrimination. Eligibility is not determined based on any prohibited factor." READ — [Handbook RSO chapter item 8](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf); [Constitution Template Art. II §B](https://campusactivities.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026-27-RSO-Constitution-Template-1.pdf)
- Event publicity is also expected to carry an accessibility statement. READ — [Handbook App. V "Accessibility"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- "The use of third-party promoters, including student brand ambassadors, is strictly prohibited." Posting is banned in 19 listed places or manners (including trees, ground, buildings, statues, lamp posts, fences, bicycle racks, sandwich boards, chalk, on top of other postings, cars); removal costs are charged to the RSO. READ — [Handbook Appendix IX](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- Advertising must not state or imply USC sponsorship; the RSO must be the primary name on all materials for co-sponsored events. READ — [T&C §I, §Q](https://trojanevents.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/TES-Terms-and-Conditions-2026.pdf)
- Logos: RSOs may create their own logos but may not alter university logos, may not use academic-unit logotypes without that unit's permission, and may not use the Interlocking SC, Baseball Interlocking SC, Football Helmet Trojan Head or the Marching Band helmet without permission from Athletics or The Spirit of Troy. Products with USC marks must come from licensed vendors. READ — [Handbook "Student Organization Logos Policy"](https://oec.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Student-Handbook-2026-August-25-002.pdf)
- The constitution must state the club is "a recognized student organization at the University of Southern California, but is not part of the University itself" and may call itself "an organization at USC, but not as part of USC itself". READ — [Constitution Template Art. VI](https://campusactivities.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026-27-RSO-Constitution-Template-1.pdf)
- Merchandise: the club needs no licence but the manufacturer must be a USC licensee, whether or not items are sold; pre-approval of a design is optional and final approval happens only when the licensed vendor submits artwork; items with no USC reference (no colours, building images, names or marks) need no licensed vendor. No lead times are published. FETCHED — [Trademarks and Licensing: USC-branded products](https://trademarks.usc.edu/?p=80)
- The authorised-vendor list for student organisations (updated 23 Apr 2025) names six vendors (USC Bookstore for ad specialties, Geneologie, Manhattan Stitching, Row Apparel, Ultimate Shirt Company, The Promotions Dept.; BSN Sports is for club sports only); USC marks may be used only with the organisation's name and/or an event it hosts. FETCHED — [Authorized Vendors – Student Organizations](https://trademarks.usc.edu/?p=384)
- Marshall adds a school layer: any item with the Marshall or USC name must be ordered through MarshallWear. FETCHED — [Marshall SOOR branding](https://clubs.marshall.usc.edu/soor/usc-marshall-logos/)
- GSG tells funded clubs to limit paper flyers and use social media, QR codes and EngageSC or Eventbrite. FETCHED — [GSG Event Planning Resources](https://gsg.usc.edu/resources/event-planning-resources/)

### Inferences
- A flyer checker can deterministically test for presence of: organisation name, date/time/place, cost, the non-discrimination sentence (either accepted wording), an accommodations contact, the word "raffle", alcohol as the focus, and "sponsored by USC" phrasing; and use vision to flag probable restricted marks (Interlocking SC) as "needs a human look". It can also generate the missing sentence, alt text and an Instagram caption. This maps directly onto two TES violation tick-boxes ("unapproved flyers", "unapproved marketing").
- Timing rule worth encoding: flyer release date must be after venue confirmation and after payments/approvals clear.
- Logo detection will produce false positives and negatives; keep it yellow, never red/green.

### Gaps
- Share of events listed on EngageSC versus Instagram: no measurement found. The RSS feed is about 1.37 million characters and its first 100,000 held 23 events, so the feed plausibly holds a few hundred events, but that is an extrapolation, not a count.
- Trademarks approval turnaround; whether TES stamps flyers; listserv guidelines; GSG's "Publicity Guidelines for Funded Programs" page (exists, last modified 2023, not opened).

## 4. Recruitment and member management ("recruit members")

### Takeaway
USC's written rule is now stricter than the 2024 press coverage implied: applications, interviews, GPA/major limits and required dues are listed as open-membership violations. EngageSC already covers rosters, forms, elections and attendance, so the only non-duplicative product angle is a compliance check on recruitment materials, and that is politically delicate.

## 10. Recommended product shape: main features, smaller features, officer journey, risks

### Takeaway
Keep one object at the centre — the event — and three pillars that map to the prompt's three verbs: plan and permit it (run events), fund and promote it legally (money and recruiting reach), and remember it (share resources). Everything is "AI reads, code checks, citations shown, human approves", sitting beside EngageSC rather than replacing it.

### Cited Findings
- Source facts supporting each recommendation are in sections 1-9; the product shape below is researcher judgement built on them.

### Inferences
**Main features (3)**
1. **Event Planner / Permit Navigator** (new, strongest differentiator). Plain-language event description is parsed into attributes (venue type, headcount, sound, food, alcohol, minors, tents, props, tickets/fundraising, dance, outside vendors, co-sponsors, recording). Code applies the rule table in section 1 and returns a back-planned timeline: each step with owner office, form link, due date, source quote, and "sources disagree" notes. It blocks impossible dates (first three weeks, study days/finals, alcohol outside Thu-Sat, dance under three months) and merges USG/GSG funding deadlines into one .ics export.
2. **Money check** (existing Greenlight funding pre-check, widened). USG application pre-check as designed; plus a fund finder (USG board, GSG, school council, FMS waiver, gift account, in-kind sponsor) and a sponsorship/fundraising legality check (cash sponsorship, activations, raffles, charity letter, ticket-sale purpose, Bookstore approval for item sales).
3. **Club Memory and Handover**. Automatic archive of every checked document, plan, quote and outcome; search and Q&A over the club's own history; generated handover packet per role; recognition-readiness tracker (constitution check, 15 officer items, advisor items, key dates such as 22 Nov and 1 Dec).

**Smaller features (4-6)**
- Constitution checker against the public 2026-27 template (demo opener; live window closes 22 Nov 2026).
- Flyer and post checker (vision): required sentences, name/date/place/cost, accommodations line, banned words and claims, probable restricted marks, release-timing rule; one-click compliant caption and alt text.
- Merch check: USC reference present? Then licensed-vendor list and Marshall/MarshallWear routing.
- Rule-change watcher using the WordPress `modified` field, with old/new diff and affected saved plans.
- Reviewer and liaison view: pre-checked queue plus aggregated failure reasons mapped to the TES checklist and recognition denial reasons.
- Optional: recruitment-material check against the four open-membership violations; date-clash and co-host suggestion from the EngageSC RSS feed; anonymised vendor/quote library across clubs.

**Unifying officer journey.** A newly elected programming chair opens the club's handover packet, sees last year's culture night (cost, vendors, what USG denied). She types "Spring culture night, about 150 people, McCarthy Quad, DJ, catered food, ticketed to raise money for a relief charity, early April". The planner returns the dated permit path (space request, UEPA, DPS, Campus Activities fundraising meeting, charity letter, amplified-sound approval, FMS, payment at 21 days) and warns that a USG vendor request must be filed six academic weeks ahead. She uploads quotes; the money check flags the cash sponsor and the over-cap catering and suggests fixes. She drops in the flyer; the checker adds the missing non-discrimination sentence and blocks posting until the venue is confirmed. After the event the evaluation reminder fires, and everything is saved for next year's board and for the renewal tracker.

**Top risks**
- Wrong "green" on a rule that is discretionary (DPS staffing, insurance, amplified sound outside Hahn Plaza): show these as "office decides — contact by date X", never as pass.
- Conflicting or stale official sources (2 vs 3 weeks; two UEPA links; 2023 dance document): apply the stricter rule, cite both, date-stamp every rule.
- Ownership: event rules belong to TES and Campus Activities, not USG/GSG. Adoption pitch should run through the USG RSO liaisons and GSG finance directors, with TES/Campus Activities as rule verifiers; Campus Activities has vacancies.
- Breadth: three pillars can look unfocused in a three-minute demo; show one journey, keep rosters, check-in, elections and email explicitly out of scope as EngageSC territory.
- Data gaps that need a mentor on the day: GSG guidebook (login-gated), RSO Toolkit (USC login), approved-caterer list (phone only), film licensing, contract signing, domestic travel, academic calendar.
- The recruitment check and the staff analytics view could be read as surveillance of clubs; keep club data private to the club and aggregate anything shown to staff.
- Privacy and security: rosters, traveller details and quotes are uploaded; treat uploads as data only, store minimal personal data.

### Gaps
- No user validation of this feature set with USC officers or staff; problem size for events remains unmeasured (no violation or denial counts are public).
